## Questions and Answers

### 1. What is the scientific name of the plant, and what is its plastid genome accession number?

- The scientific name of the selected plant is *Ocimum basilicum*. Its plastid genome accession number is NC_035143.1.

### 2. What is the total length of the plastid genome?

- The total length of the *Ocimum basilicum* plastid genome is 152,407 base pairs (bp).

### 3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on the map? Describe their positions.

- Yes. The four major structural regions can be identified on the *Ocimum basilicum* plastid genome map. The LSC region occupies the largest portion of the upper part of the circular genome. The SSC region is located in the lower portion of the map. The two inverted repeat regions, IRa and IRb, are located between the LSC and SSC regions on the lower-right and lower-left sides of the map, respectively.

### 4. Name three genes located in the LSC region.

- Three examples of genes located in the LSC region are *rbcL, psbA,* and *rpoA*.

### 5. Name at least one gene located in the SSC region.

- One example of a gene located in the SSC region is *ndhF*.

### 6. Identify one gene located within an inverted repeat. Is it shown more than once due to the duplicated IRs?

- An example of a gene located within an inverted repeat region is *ycf2*. Yes, the gene is shown more than once because the plastid genome contains two duplicated inverted-repeat regions, IRa and IRb.

### 7. Identify one photosynthesis-related gene and briefly describe its function.

- One photosynthesis-related gene visible on the map is *rbcL*. It encodes the large subunit of the enzyme Rubisco, which plays an important role in carbon fixation during photosynthesis.

### 8. What does the direction of the gene arrows mean?

- The direction of the gene arrows indicates the direction in which the genes are transcribed. Genes with arrows pointing in one direction are transcribed from one DNA strand, while genes pointing in the opposite direction are transcribed from the other strand. This shows that plastid genes can be located on both DNA strands and can have different transcriptional orientations.

### 9. Does the GC graph appear uniform or variable? Describe what you see without over-interpreting.

- The GC content does not appear completely uniform across the entire plastid genome. The graph shows fluctuations in GC content at different positions around the genome, with some regions appearing relatively higher and others relatively lower. However, the overall pattern does not show an extreme difference between regions. The graph therefore indicates local variation in GC content rather than a completely uniform distribution.

### 10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with a raw sequence or annotation list?

- A graphical plastid genome map provides a visual overview of the genome that is much easier to interpret than a raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations. It allows the locations of the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions to be seen immediately and shows where genes are distributed around the circular genome. It also makes it easier to recognize gene orientation, duplicated genes within the inverted repeats, different functional gene groups, and the overall GC content pattern. Therefore, the map helps researchers understand the overall structure and organization of the plastid genome more quickly.
